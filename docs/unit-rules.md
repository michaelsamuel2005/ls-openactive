## 2. Report rules (the 60%)

- **30 pages MAXIMUM**, counted from the start of the first chapter to the end of the last chapter. **Excluded from the count:** front matter, contents pages, declaration, bibliography/references, appendices, "tables" **[CONFIRM: whether "tables" means list-of-tables pages or tables within chapters — plan conservatively: in-chapter tables and figures COUNT]**.
- **Penalty: 5 marks per page over.** At 60% weight this is brutal — the page budget in `report_skeleton.md` is now load-bearing.
- No minimum, but substantially short ⇒ insufficient depth.
- **A LaTeX template will be provided; LaTeX highly recommended.** → **[GET the template]** before drafting anything final; do not typeset in Word and convert later.
- Referencing: "a consistent referencing system, such as the IEEE"; cite in text; no footnote referencing; quotes in quotation marks with page numbers; reference list contains ONLY cited works (it is not a bibliography). **[CONFIRM: our decision log says Harvard; the handbook's example is IEEE and the LaTeX template may ship IEEE style. Whichever is chosen must be flawless — the marking exemplar lost real marks on reference formatting. Supersede the Harvard convention in the decision log if IEEE is confirmed.]**
- Structure expectations: chapters each with a short intro and summary; abstract; introduction (RQs, aims, rationale, roadmap); research design/methods chapter that *justifies* choices and reflects on plan-vs-actual; literature review that critiques rather than describes (source-quality hierarchy matters); findings + analysis explicitly linked back to the literature and revisiting the RQs; conclusions with no new themes. An optional executive summary may be produced for the industry partner.
- Appendices: supervisor advises contents; original data must be *available to examiners on request* (not necessarily included).
- **Turnitin** similarity checking applies.

## 3. Supervision — cadence and the ONE-DRAFT rule

- Per month: **1 supervisor meeting + 2 TA meetings** (+ at most 1 optional ad-hoc supervisor meeting for urgent issues — agree the flagging mechanism with Dalila). Expect ~3 supervisor + 5–6 TA meetings total, in person by default, and about one substantial supervisor email per month.
- **Feedback on exactly ONE full draft.** Requires **≥ 14 days** lead time AND a **pre-agreed submission date**. Miss either and feedback is at risk.
  - **Arithmetic:** submission 4 Sep ⇒ draft to Dalila by **~14 August at the latest** (feedback ~28 Aug, one week to act, overlapping presentation window). **Action now: agree the draft date with Dalila at the next meeting.** This is the single hardest date in the project after the deadline itself.
- TAs monitor **engagement and group contributions** during the three months — visible, distributed repo activity is being watched, not just assessed at the end.
- **Partner contact:** never direct; questions route through TA/supervisor, who forwards to the partner. (Confirms the existing rule.)

## 4. Individual reflective account — mandatory components (NEW, affects everyone NOW)

Max 2 pages A4, min 500 words, and it **must contain**:
1. **A visualisation of the individual's project activity from the GitHub repository/log data.** ⇒ every member needs real, attributable repo activity — a member with no commits has nothing to visualise. (Tooling to generate per-member activity charts from `git log` is legitimate shared infrastructure; the reflections themselves are individual work that no one drafts for anyone.)
2. **A weekly log of activities/tasks.** ⇒ if not already kept, every member starts one TODAY and back-fills honestly. Michael's AI-assistance ledger feeds his; the unit's AI rules make that ledger doubly important.
3. Personal reflections: what worked, what didn't, what to improve, technical + non-technical learning.
